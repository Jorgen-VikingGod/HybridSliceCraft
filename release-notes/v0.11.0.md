**HybridSliceCraft 0.11.0** adds mixed filaments: colours made by alternating a few filaments layer by layer, the way full-spectrum printing does it. It is made for printers with several tool heads and is useful for ornamental prints.

## How it works

Print two or three filaments in a repeating pattern of layers, for example red, yellow, yellow, and the eye blends the thin layers into one colour, here an orange. A pattern of layers is called a mixed filament, and you use it like any filament.

## Making a mix

Under the filaments in the settings panel there is a new "Mixed filaments" list. "Add a mix" opens an editor with three tabs:

- **Ratio**: say how much of the layers each filament gets, and the program builds a short, evenly spread pattern.
- **Cycle**: click the filaments in the order they should print, such as 1-2-2-4.
- **Match**: pick a colour or enter a hex code, and the program finds the blend of your loaded filaments that looks closest. You can set the least share each filament must have. It tells you when no blend gets close, or when that minimum kept it from a closer one.

"Add all 50/50 blends" makes one mix for every pair of the filaments in use. The colour shown for a mix is an estimate; real filaments blend differently, so test with a small print.

## Using a mix

Mixes appear next to the filaments in the paint panel. Paint them on a model with the brush, the fill or "Paint everything", and the 3D view shows them in their blended colour. How far the colour reaches into the part is set by the usual "Colour reaches in" slider, so a thin shell can be mixed while the inside stays one filament.

When you slice, each mix prints one filament per layer, with a prime tower purge at every change. The program warns you when a pattern is tall enough to show as stripes, when adaptive layer heights are on (the pattern repeats by layer), and when the printer has only one tool head.

## Tips

- Use thin layers, 0.08 to 0.12 mm, and short patterns of two to four layers: a pattern taller than about 0.2 mm can be seen as stripes.
- Translucent filament blends better than very opaque filament, especially black.
- Tool heads that are slightly out of line show up as uneven colour, so calibrate the offsets of your tool changer.

## Also changed

- The prime tower now turns on whenever the filament changes between layers, not only within a layer. Before, a print where one model ended and another began on a later layer could change filament without a purge.
- The delete buttons in a few panels showed an empty box; they now show a cross.

## Good to know

- Colours that gradually change from one filament to another over the height of a part are not included yet.
- Prints that use no mixes produce the same G-code as before.

None of the saved files have been tried on a real printer yet. Check a small print first.
