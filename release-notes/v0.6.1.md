**HybridSliceCraft 0.6.1** makes slicing for filament printers about twice as fast.

## Faster filament slicing

Slicing the Stanford dragon (318,000 triangles, 1,211 layers at 0.08 mm, 143 × 63 × 97 mm) now takes about 4.1 s, down from 8.4 s. With automatic supports it takes about 7.9 s, down from 11.9 s. Tree supports take about 4.5 s. Those are slicing times alone, measured on a 20-thread processor.

- **Simpler layer outlines**: points that lie within 0.01 mm of the line between their neighbours are dropped before planning. On the dragon this halves the number of points, so every later step has less to do.
- **Faster G-code writing**: numbers are written with a faster routine that produces exactly the same text.
- **Supports are planned alongside the walls and infill** instead of afterwards. Their outlines are also simplified, by up to 0.02 mm.

## What changes in the G-code

Because outlines are simplified, the G-code is slightly different: about 7% smaller on the dragon, with the estimated print time within 1%. Tree supports and the number formatting give exactly the same output as before.

## New

- **Benchmark mode for filament**: `hybridslicecraft --bench-fdm <model>` slices without opening a window and prints the time, peak memory and a checksum of the G-code. It takes `--supports`, `--tree`, `--layer-height`, `--scale`, `--runs` and `--export <file.gcode>`. Set `HSC_TIMING=1` to see where the time goes.

## Not changed

Very large smooth models with millions of triangles can still take a long time to slice for filament printers. None of the saved files have been tried on a real printer yet. Check a small print first.
